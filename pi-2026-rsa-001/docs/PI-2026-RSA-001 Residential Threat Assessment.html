01 Executive Summary

TIER 3 · MODERATE · COMPOSITE 36 / 100

The residence is low risk and the run is where the risk sits. Residential and ambient-neighborhood exposure both score in the LOW band, and the composite is carried almost entirely by one activity-specific vulnerability: solo, pre-dawn, fixed-route running in full darkness across a seam between two patrol jurisdictions. Section 05 demonstrates this arithmetically. Holding every other input constant and removing the pre-dawn run drops the composite to 26, which is Tier 4 LOW. No verified violent incident occurred on the Lake Harriet parkway inside the assessment window, and that is an absence of reporting rather than a certification of safety.

The jurisdictional split is the single most consequential fact on this page. The residence sits under MPD. The running surface does not. Lake Harriet and its parkways are Park Board property patrolled by MPRB Park Police, whose Park Patrol Agents concentrate on lakes and parkways during summer months (MPRB, Park Police and Safety). A pre-dawn run in winter therefore sits at the thinnest point of both coverages at once. PI assesses this seam as the structural driver behind the Section 05 score, and notes that no data request addressed to MPD alone will describe it.

03 Threat Environment

Statewide and citywide trend lines point down. Two categories point up, and they are the two that matter here. Minnesota violent crime fell to 226 per 100,000 in 2025, the lowest rate since 2019 (Minnesota Bureau of Criminal Apprehension, Uniform Crime Report 2025). The Bureau subsequently issued an amended 2025 report revising statewide murders from 130 to 132 after Brooklyn Park homicides and Champlin aggravated assaults went unreported in the original filing (Minnesota Department of Public Safety, Sep 2026). Cite the amended figures. Minneapolis recorded roughly 50 fewer shootings and one third fewer robberies in 2025 against 2024 (Star Tribune, 2026).

07 Key Analytical Finds

  • A misdated statistic was withdrawn. Rev A carried a Southwest Voices figure, "5 of 7 carjackings ringing Lake Harriet occurred Nov 14 to 25," as in-window. It dates to November 2021, roughly four years outside the window. Corroborated by Axios Twin Cities (9 Nov 2021), MinnPost (Nov 2021) and the Star Tribune 2021 carjackings-by-neighborhood map. Tagged [S] rather than [V]: the byline could not be inspected directly. Full detail in Annex A.
  • Enforcement will not resolve the property-crime pattern. Minnesota's "Raise the Age" law took effect 1 Aug 2026. Children under 13 cannot be charged, prosecuted or detained, with no exceptions (Star Tribune; CBS Minnesota, Jun to Aug 2026). Offenders in the local sprees have included 11-year-olds. PI assesses the pattern as persistent for the foreseeable window.
  • The property-crime pattern does not threaten a runner, and was not permitted to inflate Category C. The 02:00 to 07:00 overlap is scored at 28 as an encounter consideration. These offenders target parked vehicles and disperse from people. The correct response to an encounter is distance and disengagement, never confrontation.
  • An activity-matched in-window assault occurred elsewhere in the metro. A lone woman was assaulted on an early-morning trail inside Elm Creek Park Reserve on 31 Aug 2026 and the suspect was not apprehended (Hoodline, Aug 2026). Maple Grove is approximately 15 miles northwest, a different jurisdiction and a different park system. It contributes nothing to this location's base rate and is excluded from Section 05. It is recorded because it matches the activity profile being scored.
  • One counterweight cuts against the darkness assumption. National reporting citing Federal Bureau of Investigation framing notes that assaults on runners frequently occur in daylight and taper in early-morning hours. PI retains this rather than discarding it. It is national and general, and it is not evidence about this parkway.
