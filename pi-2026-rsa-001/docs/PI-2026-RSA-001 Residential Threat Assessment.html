The jurisdictional split is the most consequential fact in this assessment. The residence sits under the Minneapolis Police Department. The running surface does not. Lake Harriet and its parkways are Park Board property patrolled by MPRB Park Police, whose Park Patrol Agents concentrate on lakes and parkways during summer months (MPRB, Park Police and Safety). No data request addressed to MPD alone will describe it, which is why Section 09 calls for two.

Two tests, both passed. Scope test: Rev B scored 36 with citywide and statewide inputs carrying part of Category B. Rev C removed all of them and re-based B on neighborhood evidence alone. The composite held at 36. A score that survives the removal of its broadest inputs rests on the local record, not on background noise. Sensitivity test: hold A and B constant and reduce C to its ambient environmental residue of roughly 18, which is what remains once activity exposure, route predictability, construction and the offender window fall away and the patrol seam stops applying to a resident indoors at 05:30. The composite becomes (0.25 × 31) + (0.32 × 32) + (0.43 × 18) = 26, which is Tier 4 LOW. One behavior moves this assessment across a tier boundary. Nothing else in the matrix does.

07 Neighborhood Sentiment

The lake reads as safe and busy, and the dominant public complaint is crowding rather than danger. That is a materially different signal from a corridor residents avoid, and it is the strongest sentiment finding available from public sources. The Lake Harriet loop trail carries more than 1,600 public reviews, with usage described as heavy.

Against that, coverage of the break-in wave captured the tension in the ring neighborhoods directly:

"We think we live in a bubble here, but I guess not." Linden Hills resident, following a neighborhood break-in

PI assesses this as a meaningful negative indicator: residents organising around park amenities and construction, rather than around safety, is what a low-threat neighborhood looks like from the inside. 08 Key Analytical Finds

  • Three separate claims about this neighborhood proved misdated. A carjacking cluster presented in Rev A as in-window is from November 2021. An MPD 5th Precinct aggravated-robbery warning describing victims "alone or with one other person, either walking," which reads as directly on point, cites Inspector Katie Blackwell, who left that command in August 2023. A search summary returned the band shell shooting as "October 2026" when the charging documents place it at 24 October 2023. All three were excluded. PI assesses date verification, not collection volume, as the binding constraint on this assessment.
  • A vehicular threat vector was missing from the prior revision. The 1 May 2026 East Harriet collision put a stolen car through a stop sign at 80 mph with its lights off, on residential streets inside the neighborhood, at night. The November 2020 parkway strike shows the same mechanism reaching a runner on the exact surface in question. This is now scored in Category B at 45.
  • Construction is active on the route corridor right now. Storm sewer reconstruction on Oliver Avenue between W 50th Street and Lake Harriet Parkway began 13 July 2026 and runs into summer 2027. It changes footing, lighting and available line of sight on part of the route for the entire coming winter, which is the season the pre-dawn run is most exposed. The physical recon at Section 09 should be walked with this in place.
  • The property-crime pattern still does not threaten a runner, and Category C was not inflated on its account. The 02:00 to 07:00 overlap stays scored at 28 as an encounter consideration. Those offenders target parked vehicles and disperse from people. Distance and disengagement, never confrontation.
