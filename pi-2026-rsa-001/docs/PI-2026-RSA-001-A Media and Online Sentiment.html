Correction notice — read first

Rev A contained a misdated statistic. Rev A is withdrawn.

Withdrawn claim: "5 of the 7 carjackings in the neighborhoods ringing Lake Harriet occurred between November 14 and 25" — presented in Rev A as an in-window figure.

Finding: the statistic is from November 2021, roughly four years outside the assessment window. It should never have appeared in a trailing-365-day product.

Basis for the correction. The originating Southwest Voices piece dates its own comparison cluster to "a ten-day stretch of November 2021" and cites Kingfield carjackings on 8, 14, 15 and 16 November. Three independently dated sources place the surrounding carjacking wave in the same period: Axios Twin Cities (9 Nov 2021), MinnPost "Carjackings up 38% in Minneapolis in 2021" (Nov 2021), and the Star Tribune's 2021 carjackings-by-neighborhood map. [S]

Residual uncertainty, stated plainly. The Southwest Voices article itself could not be opened from this environment — outbound access to southwestvoices.news is blocked by network policy. The 2021 dating therefore rests on corroborating dated sources, not on direct inspection of the byline. Confidence is high but the tag is [S], not [V]. Direct verification of that byline remains an open item.

Net effect on the assessment: no carjacking cluster is attributable to the assessment window. The overnight vehicle-break-in pattern is a separate finding, independently verified, in-window, and unaffected by this correction.

Public-web sentiment [A]

The path reads as safe and busy. The recurring public complaint about Lake Harriet is crowding, not danger — a materially different signal from a corridor residents avoid. Against that, coverage of the break-in wave captured the tension directly:

"We think we live in a bubble here, but I guess not." — Linden Hills resident, following a neighborhood break-in

One national piece (NBC, citing FBI framing) notes that assaults on runners frequently occur in daylight and taper in the early-morning hours — a genuine counterweight to darkness-based intuition, and one this assessment does not discard. It cuts against, not for, the pre-dawn concern. It is also national and general; it is not evidence about this parkway.

Capture rule for every hit: record the date and whether the account is firsthand or repost/rumor. Those two fields decide the weight. In-window and firsthand → retain. Older than the window, or hearsay → discard. Applying this rule at collection time is what prevents a repeat of the carjacking error above.
