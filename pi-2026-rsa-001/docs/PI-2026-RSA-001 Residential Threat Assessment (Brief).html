The neighborhood is safe. The run is the risk. Across the last year, nothing violent was reported anywhere on the Lake Harriet parkway. Eighteen events turned up within five miles of the house, and the closest one of any kind was a car crash about a mile away. Almost the entire score comes from one thing: running alone, in the dark, on the same route at the same time, in a spot where two different police departments each cover only part of the ground. Take away the pre-dawn run and the score drops to 27, which is LOW.

The run, in plain terms

  • The darkness is the variable, not the lake. In September a 5:30 start is close to first light. By November and through March it is fully dark for the entire run. The route does not change; the risk does, month to month.
  • Being predictable is the one thing fully in your control. Same route, same time, every day is the single factor you can change today that most reduces exposure. Vary the start by 20 minutes and alternate direction.
  • The biggest realistic hazard is a car, not a person. The 1 May crash is the clearest signal in the whole file: a stolen car at 80 mph, lights off, running a stop sign on residential streets a mile from your door. City police no longer chase stolen cars unless a violent felony is involved, so these vehicles keep moving. Run facing traffic where you can, and treat every intersection as though a car may arrive without lights.
  • If you come across someone breaking into a car, keep going. A man four miles from here died in May doing the opposite. Distance and disengagement, never confrontation, and never a photograph from close range.
  • There is construction on your route right now. Oliver Ave between W 50th and the parkway is dug up until summer 2027. Uneven footing and changed lighting, through the darkest months.

Two things this brief will not tell you

"Nothing was reported" is not "nothing happened." No violent incident turned up on the parkway in the last year, and that search was run repeatedly and several different ways. It still only means nothing reached a source we can read. The city's own incident-level crime data would settle it properly and could not be reached from this environment. Treat the clean record as encouraging, not as proof.

The one-year limit hides the closest match. In September 2015 a runner was grabbed from behind and chased at 5:30 a.m. on West Lake Harriet Parkway. Same time, same place, same activity. It falls outside the year reviewed, so it is not counted here. There were also a string of incidents on this lake in 2017 and a jogger struck by a car on this parkway in 2020. Those are the most relevant events that exist for this question, and the one-year rule removes all of them.

Checks worth running before you treat this as settled

  • Walk the route once at 5:30 a.m., this season. Nobody can do this from a database. Look at lighting, blind spots, how isolated the darkest stretch feels, whether your phone has signal, and how the Oliver Ave construction changes things. A pleasant summer walk does not answer a January question.
  • Request the police records. Two of them, not one. The city police cover your house. The Park Board police cover the parkway you actually run on. A request to the city alone misses the running surface entirely. Both drafts are written and ready to send, and the Park Board one asks how many patrol hours the lake actually gets between 4 and 7 a.m.
  • Search Nextdoor and Reddit yourself. Neither can be reached from here: Nextdoor needs your login, and Reddit blocks this kind of automated access outright. Your neighbors' accounts are the one source that would show incidents too small to make the news. For anything you find, note the date and whether the person saw it themselves.
  • Run the two offender-registry searches against the address. Both are quick interactive lookups.
