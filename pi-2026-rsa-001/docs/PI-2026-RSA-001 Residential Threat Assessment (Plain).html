PI Assessment
This is a safe neighborhood. Theft — smashed car windows and stolen packages — is the everyday crime here, not violence. In the last 12 months we found no reported assaults, robberies, or other violent crimes on or beside the Lake Harriet path. Eighteen events met the bar within five miles, and the nearest of any kind was a car crash about a mile away.
The risk in this report is not the house. It is the 5:30 a.m. solo run — alone, in the dark, on a quiet and unlit stretch of the lake. That habit, not the neighborhood, is what makes this Moderate instead of Low. Run after sunrise and the rating drops to Low.

The Area
Neighborhood
The home sits on the Lynnhurst / East Harriet border, across the parkway from Lake Harriet. The surrounding ring — East Harriet, Lynnhurst, Fulton, and Linden Hills — is one of the quieter, wealthier parts of Minneapolis. City of Minneapolis, Ward 13
Who Patrols What
The house and the running path belong to two different police departments. Minneapolis Police (5th Precinct) cover the home; the Park Police cover the lake, and they focus on the lakes mostly in summer. Before sunrise in winter, both are at their quietest — help will not arrive fast. MPD 5th Precinct / MPRB
The Property Itself
The home is publicly listed for sale at $4,450,000, with interior photos and a floor plan viewable by anyone online. A high-value, openly advertised house on a visible parkway is a burglary target on paper — keep valuables out of sight and lock up during showings. MLS #7050178; Coldwell Banker / Redfin, 2026

Walking & Running — What Matters Near the Home
1
On Foot
Fast, unlit vehicles — not people
HIGH
The one serious incident this year was a stolen car running a stop sign at 80 mph before dawn. Police no longer chase stolen cars unless a violent felony is involved, so these vehicles keep moving. The break-in crews work at night, target parked cars, and stay away from people.
2
Before Sunrise
Thin police coverage at 05:30
HIGH
Park Police coverage of the lake is lightest in winter mornings, and it is a different department from the one that covers the home. Do not count on help arriving fast if something happens in the dark.
3
All Winter
Construction on the route
WATCH
The Oliver Avenue and W 50th Street sewer work runs into 2027. Expect dark stretches, detours, and uneven ground on part of the route through the dark months.
4
Predictable
Same route, same time, every day
WATCH
A fixed 05:30 run on a fixed route is the easiest thing to change — and the change that lowers this report's score the most. Vary the day, the time, and the direction.
5
Break-in crews
They avoid people — don't confront
LOW
The window-smash crews disperse from people. A man four miles from here died in May after confronting two people stealing his car. If you cross paths with one: distance and disengagement, never confrontation.

What To Do
REC 01
Running
Move the 05:30 run past sunrise. This single change takes the rating from Moderate (37) to Low (27). Nothing else moves the score this much.
REC 02
Running
If you run in the dark anyway: lit streets next to houses, not the unlit lakeside and band shell stretches. Phone and ID on you; someone knows your route and return time. Light and reflective kit; one ear free of headphones; face traffic.
REC 03
Running
Break the routine. Change the route and start time day to day, and use the posted walking detours around the construction on Oliver Avenue and W 50th Street.
REC 04
The Home
De-risk showings while the home is listed. Agent-escorted tours only; valuables locked or off-site; no lockbox access left unsupervised.
REC 05
The Home
Close the garage gap. Deadbolt garage-to-house doors, shield release cords, remotes out of parked cars, and a camera on each garage and the parkway approach.
REC 06
Anytime
If approached: distance and light. Move toward houses, call 911. For smashed cars or suspicious idling vehicles, use 612-348-2345 — reports are what build the pattern record.

What the 12-Month Rule Leaves Out
Read This
Holding to the last 12 months removes the closest thing on record to the exact question being asked. In September 2015 a solo runner was grabbed from behind and chased at 5:30 a.m. on West Lake Harriet Parkway near Sheridan Avenue South — same time, same place, same activity. The rule also removes a run of escalating incidents on this lake in July 2017, and a jogger struck by a car on this parkway in November 2020. Those three are the most relevant events that exist for this location. The 12-month picture is less informative about the question, not safer. WCCO / CBS Minnesota; Star Tribune
And This
"Nothing reported" is not "nothing happened." The clean record on the lake path means no incident reached a source we can read. It is not proof none occurred, and it cannot become proof until the police records requests come back. The city's own incident-level crime data would settle it and could not be reached during this work. Treat the clean record as encouraging, not as a guarantee.
